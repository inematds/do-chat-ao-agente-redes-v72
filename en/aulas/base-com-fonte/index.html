Lesson 4 · 18 min

The sourced base: every number with the literal excerpt

The base.md is the heart of the channel with a source. Every number that goes on screen goes in with who said it, the video, the date, and an excerpt copied from the transcript, unedited. A script checks each excerpt; if any is not there, the video is discarded. Here you build your own base and your own checker.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1From the video to the transcript, on your computer
  2. 2The five columns of the base.md
  3. 3What a literal excerpt is (and what it is not)
  4. 4A 15-line checker, like checar.py

Goal: build the base.md for a video in your niche, with a literal excerpt on each line, and check each excerpt against the transcript with a script.

01 · From the video to the checked line

Click each block.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

The check does not judge whether the number is true in the world: it guarantees that someone said exactly that, in the cited video. That is why everything is attributed ("according to him").

02 · Literal excerpt: right and wrong

Illustrative examples, from an invented transcript for the real estate niche.

1. Right

Copy straight from the transcript, just as it is, even if the speech is loose. Between 6 and 30 words, enough to prove the number.

Excerpt"o aluguel médio aqui na região subiu doze por cento em um ano"

2. Wrong: edited

Swapping words to make it sound nicer breaks the check. The script can't find it and the video is dropped.

Excerpt"o valor médio do aluguel na região aumentou 12% no último ano"

3. Wrong: pasted together

Joining the start of one sentence with the end of another creates a sentence nobody said.

Excerpt"o aluguel médio subiu… e vai continuar subindo em dois mil e vinte e sete"

4. Wrong: too short

Two or three words show up anywhere and don't show the context. Use at least 6.

Excerpt"twelve percent"

confere.py
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""usage: python3 confere.py base.md transcript.txt
Is each quoted excerpt in the base found in the transcript? Exits 1 if any is missing."""
import re, sys, unicodedata

def n(s):  # lowercase, no accents, no punctuation
    s = unicodedata.normalize("NFD", s.lower()).encode("ascii", "ignore").decode()
    return re.sub(r"\s+", " ", re.sub(r"[^a-z0-9%]+", " ", s)).strip()

base = open(sys.argv[1], encoding="utf-8").read()
tx = n(open(sys.argv[2], encoding="utf-8").read())
fora = 0
for trecho in re.findall(r'"([^"]{20,})"', base):
    ok = n(trecho) in tx
    fora += not ok
    print("OK  " if ok else "FORA", trecho[:70])
sys.exit(1 if fora else 0)
Simulation: illustrative example
OK   average rent in the region rose twelve percent in a year
FORA the average rent value in the region increased 12% in the last year

A FORA line: fix it by copying again from the transcript. The checar.py in the radars does the same for each video cited.

The transcript and the base can differ in accents, commas, or capitalization without the meaning changing. Normalizing both sides avoids failing a line for no reason, and it still catches a swapped word or a pasted sentence.
